What does "detox" actually mean?

Detoxification is the biological process by which your body neutralises, processes and eliminates harmful substances — including alcohol, medications, environmental pollutants, and metabolic by-products. It is not a single event you can complete in seven days. It is a continuous, multi-organ process that has been happening every second since you were born.

The marketing term "detox" — usually shorthand for a juice cleanse, herbal tea or supplement kit — is a different concept entirely. The UK's National Center for Complementary and Integrative Health and the British Medical Journal have both noted that there is no agreed clinical definition of a commercial "detox", and very few studies that show these products remove any identifiable toxin from the body.

Detox supplements in New Zealand: what's on the shelf?

Walk into any New Zealand pharmacy or supermarket and you'll see four broad categories of "detox" products:

  1. Liver-support capsules — usually milk thistle, dandelion root, turmeric, or globe artichoke
  2. Cleanse kits — multi-day programmes combining laxatives, fibre and herbal extracts
  3. Detox teas — often containing senna or other stimulant laxatives
  4. Juice cleanses and meal-replacement detox drinks — typically low-calorie, low-protein

In New Zealand, supplements are regulated as dietary supplements under the Food Act 2014 and the Dietary Supplements Regulations 1985 — overseen by Medsafe and the Ministry for Primary Industries. Manufacturers are not required to prove efficacy before sale (unlike registered medicines), which is precisely why evidence-led product selection matters.

Pharmacy tip: If a product makes a specific disease claim (e.g. "treats fatty liver"), it is likely making a non-compliant therapeutic claim. Look instead for honest, regulator-friendly language like "may support healthy liver function" or "traditionally used to support digestion".

What the science actually says

Here's the evidence on the most common detox ingredients sold in NZ pharmacies, ranked from strongest to weakest clinical support.

What the evidence supports

1. Soluble fibre (psyllium, oat beta-glucan). Multiple Cochrane reviews show soluble fibre supports regular bowel movements — which is your gut's main route for eliminating bile-bound toxins. Fibre also feeds beneficial gut bacteria. Most New Zealanders eat 15–20g of fibre per day; the Ministry of Health recommends 25–30g. A daily psyllium-based supplement like Metamucil is one of the most evidence-backed "detox" products in the entire category.

2. Probiotics. A 2022 review in the journal Nutrients found multi-strain probiotics modestly improve markers of gut barrier function and reduce circulating endotoxin in adults with metabolic syndrome. They are also the most robust evidence base in the gut–liver axis. Browse our full Digestive Health range for pharmacy-grade options.

3. Milk thistle (silymarin). The active compound in milk thistle, silymarin, has been studied extensively. NIH's Office of Dietary Supplements concludes the evidence is "promising" for liver cell protection in chronic liver conditions, though large clinical trials remain limited. Klein et al.'s widely-cited BMJ analysis of 18 trials found silymarin was well tolerated, with biochemical improvements in liver enzymes in several conditions. It will not "cleanse" a healthy liver — but it may support it.

4. Hydration. The single most underrated detox intervention. Your kidneys cannot excrete water-soluble waste without adequate fluid intake. NZ guidelines suggest around 2L of fluid per day for most adults, more in summer or with exercise.

The unsexy truth: The "supplements" with the strongest detox evidence are fibre, water, sleep and reducing alcohol — all of which cost almost nothing. Capsules and teas are at best a complement, never a replacement.

What the evidence does not support

Detox teas with senna or cascara. These work by stimulating the bowel — i.e. they are laxatives. They cause weight loss only through water and stool loss, and prolonged use can lead to dependence, electrolyte imbalance and dehydration. The 2015 BMJ analysis on commercial detox products found "no compelling evidence" of toxin removal.

Foot detox pads and ionic foot baths. Independent testing has shown the brown discolouration claimed to be "toxins" is simply the result of the chemical reaction between the pad ingredients (or bath electrodes) and water — it appears whether or not feet are in the water. There is no plausible biological mechanism by which toxins exit the foot.

Juice cleanses. A 2023 review in Critical Reviews in Food Science and Nutrition concluded most multi-day juice cleanses are low in protein and fibre, may cause blood-sugar swings, and have no demonstrated benefit beyond short-term water-weight loss.

How to choose a detox supplement: a 5-point pharmacy checklist

If you do decide a detox or liver-support supplement is right for you, here is the framework our pharmacy team uses when making recommendations:

What to checkWhat to look for
1. Active ingredient and dose Look for a named, standardised extract (e.g. "milk thistle 35,000mg equiv., standardised to 80% silymarin"). Vague "proprietary blends" hide the dose.
2. Evidence base Cross-check the active ingredient against the NIH Office of Dietary Supplements fact sheets at ods.od.nih.gov.
3. Manufacturing standard Prefer brands manufactured to GMP standard. In NZ this includes Clinicians, Blackmores, Good Health, Nutra-Life and Thompson's.
4. Honest claims Compliant language: "may support healthy liver function". Walk away from anything claiming to "cure", "flush" or "reset" an organ.
5. Interaction profile Some "detox" herbs interact with prescription medicines — especially blood thinners, oral contraceptives and immunosuppressants. Always ask a pharmacist.
Red flags to avoid: "Rapid 3-day detox", "flush your liver", "eliminate toxins overnight", undisclosed proprietary blends, products sold only via social-media influencers, and anything that requires you to fast for more than 24 hours without medical supervision.

Beyond supplements: the five lifestyle levers that really matter

If you only do four things from this article, do these.

  1. Drink more water. Aim for around 2 litres a day. Your kidneys cannot excrete water-soluble waste without it.
  2. Eat more fibre. Target 25–30g a day from wholegrains, legumes, vegetables and fruit. This single change does more for your gut than any 7-day cleanse.
  3. Drink less alcohol. Alcohol is the most common preventable cause of liver damage in New Zealand. The NZ Ministry of Health recommends no more than 10 standard drinks per week for women and 15 for men, with at least two alcohol-free days.
  4. Move every day. Aerobic exercise increases bile flow, improves insulin sensitivity and reduces fatty deposits in the liver — confirmed in multiple meta-analyses.
  5. Sleep 7–9 hours. Your brain's glymphatic system — its overnight waste-clearance cycle — only runs properly during deep sleep. Related reading: our pharmacy team's guide to magnesium for sleep and relaxation.

When to talk to a pharmacist or GP first

Before starting any detox supplement, please come in and have a chat with our team — or speak to your GP — if any of the following apply to you:

  • You are pregnant or breastfeeding
  • You are taking prescription medications, especially blood thinners (warfarin, dabigatran), oral contraceptives, immunosuppressants, statins or seizure medications
  • You have a known liver, kidney or gallbladder condition
  • You are buying the product for a child under 12
  • You experience yellowing of the skin or eyes, dark urine, persistent fatigue or right-sided abdominal pain — these need medical assessment, not a supplement

Frequently asked questions

Do detox supplements really work?

Some do, in specific ways. Ingredients like milk thistle (silymarin), soluble fibre and probiotics have meaningful clinical evidence supporting liver-cell protection, regularity and gut barrier function. But no supplement "flushes" toxins out of a healthy body — your liver and kidneys already do that every second of every day.

What is the best detox supplement in New Zealand?

There isn't a single "best" — it depends on your goal. For liver support, a standardised milk thistle product like GO Healthy Milk Thistle 50,000 or Clinicians Liver Protect Plus is well evidenced. For daily gut clearance, psyllium-based fibre (Metamucil) has the strongest evidence base of any product in the category. Ask your Bargain Chemist pharmacist for a recommendation tailored to your medications.

Can milk thistle actually cleanse your liver?

No supplement "cleanses" a healthy liver — the liver cleanses itself. Milk thistle's active compound, silymarin, has antioxidant properties and may protect liver cells from oxidative damage. NIH's Office of Dietary Supplements describes the evidence as "promising" for supportive use, particularly alongside lifestyle changes.

How long should you do a detox?

Ongoing daily lifestyle support (fibre, hydration, low alcohol, sleep, exercise) is more beneficial than any short "detox" programme. If you choose to use a liver-support supplement, most NZ products are designed for daily use over 1–3 months. There is no medical reason to do a "7-day detox" — and prolonged use of laxative-based teas can be harmful.

Are detox teas safe?

Most "detox teas" sold in New Zealand contain stimulant laxatives such as senna or cascara. They are safe for occasional, short-term use as laxatives, but using them daily for weight loss or "cleansing" can cause dehydration, electrolyte imbalance, cramping and laxative dependence. They are not a safe long-term wellness habit.

What are the side effects of liver detox supplements?

Most well-formulated NZ products are well tolerated. Milk thistle can occasionally cause mild gastrointestinal upset or allergic reaction in people sensitive to the daisy family. Combined liver-detox formulas may interact with prescription medicines, so always check with your pharmacist before starting one.

Do I need a detox after drinking alcohol?

The most effective post-alcohol "detox" is water, sleep, food and time. No supplement reverses alcohol's effects on the liver. If you regularly exceed the NZ Ministry of Health's low-risk drinking guidelines (10 standard drinks per week for women, 15 for men), reducing intake is the single most beneficial intervention.

Is a juice cleanse the same as a detox?

Marketing often uses the terms interchangeably, but a juice cleanse is simply a low-calorie, low-protein, high-sugar liquid diet. It does not remove toxins. Short-term weight loss is mostly water and muscle glycogen, and is regained within days. For most healthy adults, a fibre-rich whole-food diet is more beneficial.

Should I see a pharmacist before starting a detox supplement?

Yes — especially if you take any prescription medication, are pregnant or breastfeeding, or have a chronic health condition. What are natural alternatives to detox supplements?

The most effective natural detox supports are also the cheapest: drink 2 litres of water daily, eat 25–30g of fibre, get 7–9 hours of sleep, exercise regularly, and keep alcohol within the Ministry of Health's low-risk limits. Cruciferous vegetables (broccoli, kale, cabbage) also provide compounds that naturally support liver phase II enzymes.
