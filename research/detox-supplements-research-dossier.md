## 2. Verified citations (use these in the blog)

### 2.1 Milk thistle / silymarin evidence
- **Rambaldi A, Jacobs BP, Gluud C. Milk thistle for alcoholic and/or hepatitis B or C virus liver diseases. Cochrane Database Syst Rev. 2007;(4):CD003620.** PMID 17943794. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17943794/ · Plain-language summary: https://www.cochrane.org/CD003620/LIVER_no-evidence-supporting-or-refuting-milk-thistle-for-alcoholic-andor-hepatitis-b-or-c-virus-liver-diseases
  *Finding: 13 RCTs, 915 patients. Cochrane conclusion: "no evidence supporting or refuting milk thistle for alcoholic and/or hepatitis B or C virus liver diseases" based on high-quality trials.*
- **Saller R, Brignoli R, Melzer J, Meier R. An updated systematic review with meta-analysis for the clinical evidence of silymarin. Forsch Komplementmed. 2008;15(1):9–20.** PMID 18334810. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18334810/
  *Identified 65 papers; 19 met blinding criteria. Source of the "≈18 trials" memory I previously mis-attributed to Klein/BMJ.*
- **Dhande RS, et al. Silymarin as an Antioxidant Therapy in Chronic Liver Diseases: A Comprehensive Review. Cureus. 2024.** PMID 39286715. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11404857/
  *Safety: AE rates <4% in trials, comparable to placebo (~0.67%); discontinuation ~0.68% vs 0.67% placebo. Most common AEs are headache and pruritus, each <1.5%.*
- **NCCIH (NIH) Milk Thistle Fact Sheet** (authoritative for NZ pharmacy context — NIH ODS does not have a dedicated milk thistle health-professional sheet). https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/milk-thistle
  *Finding: "There isn't enough high-quality evidence to allow definite conclusions". Trial results "conflicting or too limited" for alcohol-related, hepatitis B/C, NAFLD, chemo-induced and toxin-induced liver conditions. Small studies suggest type 2 diabetes blood-sugar control may be supported.*

### 2.2 Detox / cleanse evidence
- **Klein AV, Kiat H. Detox diets for toxin elimination and weight management: a critical review of the evidence. J Hum Nutr Diet. 2015;28(6):675–86.** PMID 25522674. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25522674/
  *NOT BMJ — the journal is Journal of Human Nutrition and Dietetics. Quote: "very little clinical evidence to support the use of detox diets" — eight commercial programs reviewed, no rigorous RCTs.*
- **NCCIH (NIH) "Detoxes and Cleanses: What You Need To Know"**. https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/detoxes-and-cleanses-what-you-need-to-know
  *FDA and FTC have taken enforcement action against detox/cleanse products for hidden ingredients, false claims, and unapproved colon-cleansing devices. Side effects more likely in people with GI disease, prior colon surgery, severe haemorrhoids, kidney or heart disease. Unpasteurised juices used in cleanses can cause foodborne illness.*
- **British Dietetic Association — Detox Diets Food Fact Sheet**. https://www.bda.uk.com/resource/detox-diets.html
  *"Marketing myth rather than nutritional reality." Replaces the retired NHS Live Well detox page.*
- **Sakaki JR, et al. Effects of Vegetable and Fruit Juicing on Gut and Oral Microbiome Composition. Nutrients. 2025;17(3):458.** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11820471/
  *Short juicing increases pro-inflammatory Proteobacteria and disrupts microbiome — direct counter to "juice cleanse" claims.*

### 2.3 Probiotics + liver
- **Xiong Y, et al. Clinical efficacy of probiotics in alcoholic liver disease: systematic review and meta-analysis. Front Cell Infect Microbiol. 2024;14:1358063.** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10964906/
  *9 RCTs. Probiotic supplementation significantly reduced ALT, AST and γ-GT in ALD patients.*
- **Sabico S, et al. Multi-strain probiotics in T2DM patients — endotoxemic, inflammatory and cardiometabolic effects (6-month RCT). Clin Nutr. 2019;38(4):1561–9.** https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0261561418313517
  *~70% reduction in circulating endotoxin in T2DM (not "metabolic syndrome" as I had said).*

## 5. 2025–2026 SEO + GEO + AEO playbook updates

Most critical updates that change the original playbook:

1. **HowTo schema is dead** (Google retired rich results Sept 2023). Remove from any legacy posts.
2. **FAQ rich results in SERPs are gone** (final retirement May 2026) — but FAQPage schema still valuable for LLMs and AI Overviews. Keep it.
3. **Schema alone does NOT cause AI citations** (Ahrefs May 2026 study: 1,885 pages vs 4,000 controls, statistically insignificant). Implement as hygiene; don't expect a silver bullet.
4. **AI Overview citations: top-10 dropped from 76% → 38% in 12 months.** Long-tail informational pages can be cited without ranking top-10.
5. **55% of AIO citations come from the top 30% of the page** — front-load the direct answer in first 100–150 words.
6. **Content <3 months old is ~3× more likely to be cited.** 85% of citations come from last 2 years. Refresh every 90–120 days.
7. **Multi-modal (text + image + video + tables) shows 156% higher AI selection rates** than text-only.
8. **YouTube is the most-cited domain in AIOs (+34% in 6 months).** Embed a short branded explainer or transcript.
9. **Princeton GEO study validated tactics**:
   - "Statistics Addition" → +41% visibility
   - "Quotation Addition" → +28%
   - "Cite Sources" → up to +115% for lower-ranked content
   - Fluency + Authoritative Voice rank top 5
10. **Different LLMs have different source biases**:
    - ChatGPT: Wikipedia ~48%
    - Perplexity: Reddit ~47%, real-time news
    - Claude: bulleted/tabular structure (30% more likely)
    - Gemini: ~54% overlap Google organic
11. **September 2025 Search Quality Rater Guidelines update** — low-effort AI-generated content explicitly downgraded to lowest quality. AI-assisted drafting must have substantive human + clinician review.
12. **December 2025 Core Update** — ~67% of YMYL health pages without physician/pharmacist authorship lost rankings on symptom/treatment queries.
13. **MedicalWebPage schema** with `lastReviewed`, `reviewedBy` strengthens YMYL signals.
14. **NZBN as schema identifier** is a 2025+ NZ-specific AI-disambiguation lever.
15. **Mobile breadcrumb display gone (Jan 2025)** but BreadcrumbList schema crawl reliance increased. Keep.
16. **Speakable schema** is BETA, US-English news only — don't deploy for NZ pharmacy.

## 6. Compliance red lines (NZ pharmacy YMYL)

For supplements on the Bargain Chemist blog, NEVER:

| ❌ Do not | Source |
|---|---|
| Claim a supplement "prevents", "cures", "treats", "alleviates" or "manages" a named disease | Medsafe Dietary Supplements regulation ; Medicines Act 1981 s.58 |
| Name disease states a supplement helps with (e.g. "for fatty liver", "for cirrhosis") | Medsafe |
| Name symptoms a supplement relieves (e.g. "stops insomnia") | Medsafe |
| Include HCP endorsements implying therapeutic benefit ("Dr Smith recommends this for liver health") | Medicines Act s.58(1)(c)(ii) |
| Publish consumer testimonials with claimed therapeutic improvement | Medicines Act s.58(1)(c)(iii) ; ASA Code |
| Use off-label clinical evidence to justify a claim | ASA Therapeutic & Health Advertising Code |
| Portray unrealistic outcomes or exploit emotional vulnerability / low health literacy | Updated ASA Code (effective 1 Apr 2026) |
| Make therapeutic claims on supplemented food | MPI / FSC 1.2.7 |
| Promote supplements without an evidence basis | Pharmacy Council Code of Ethics 2018 |

**Compliant phrasing**: "may support healthy liver function", "traditionally used to support digestion", "contains evidence-backed silymarin at a clinically studied dose" — focus on the ingredient + the evidence, not the disease + the cure.

**E-E-A-T framing for the pharmacy team byline**: write/review as an evidence-led pharmacy team; do NOT frame as "Pharmacist X recommends this product to treat [disease]". Recommend the *category of evidence-backed ingredient*, not a *therapeutic outcome*.
